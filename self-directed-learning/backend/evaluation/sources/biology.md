# Diffusion and osmosis
Diffusion is net movement arising from random molecular motion down a concentration gradient. At dynamic equilibrium, molecules still move in both directions but there is no net flux. Osmosis is net water movement across a selectively permeable membrane driven by a difference in water chemical potential.

For the simple classroom model here, solutes cannot cross the membrane and pressure differences are initially negligible. Water moves toward the side with greater effective solute concentration. An animal cell in a hypotonic solution tends to gain water and swell; in a hypertonic solution it tends to lose water and shrink. Tonicity depends on effectively nonpenetrating solutes, not simply every dissolved particle.

An isotonic environment produces no sustained net change in animal-cell volume in this model. Do not infer that individual water molecules stop moving. Plant-cell walls change mechanical consequences, so animal-cell conclusions should not be silently generalized to plant cells.
