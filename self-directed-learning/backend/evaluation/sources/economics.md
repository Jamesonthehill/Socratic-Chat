# A competitive market model
In a simple competitive market, the demand curve relates price to quantity demanded holding other influences fixed. A change in the good's own price produces movement along that curve. Changes in income, preferences or other determinants can shift demand. A change in demand is not the same as a change in quantity demanded.

Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A legally enforced price ceiling below the otherwise prevailing equilibrium price is binding in this model. At that price, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, creating a shortage. A ceiling above equilibrium is nonbinding. The shortage is the gap between quantities at the controlled price, not a shift of the curves by itself.

Predictions rely on the stated model and other conditions being fixed. A simultaneous supply shift can alter the result. Do not conclude from this model alone that a policy has identical effects in every actual market or determines all welfare consequences.
