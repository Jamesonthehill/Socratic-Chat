# Force and motion in an inertial frame
For constant mass, the vector net external force on an object equals its mass times acceleration. Velocity describes motion; acceleration describes change in velocity. Zero net force implies zero acceleration, so an object can move at constant nonzero velocity. A force need not point in the direction of the velocity.

At a given net force, greater mass means smaller acceleration. Draw a force diagram for one object and combine only forces on that object. For a cart on a level surface, vertical weight and support can balance while horizontal forces determine horizontal acceleration.

Newton's third law pairs forces between two interacting bodies: the force of a hand on a cart and the force of the cart on the hand are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, but act on different bodies. They therefore do not cancel on a force diagram of the cart alone.
