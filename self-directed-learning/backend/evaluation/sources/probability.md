# Conditional probability with natural frequencies
For events A and B with P(B) greater than zero, P(A given B) equals P(A and B) divided by P(B). Reversing the condition generally changes the denominator and the answer. Independence means P(A and B) equals P(A)P(B); mutually exclusive nonzero-probability events are not independent.

Consider a hypothetical screening test, not clinical advice. In 1000 people, 10 have a condition. Sensitivity is 90%, so about 9 of those 10 test positive. A false-positive rate of 10% among the 990 without the condition gives about 99 false positives. Among approximately 108 positive results, about 9 have the condition: roughly 8.3%, not 90%.

The prior prevalence, sensitivity and false-positive rate jointly determine the interpretation. Natural frequencies help keep the conditioned group visible. Expected counts summarize repeated use of the stated model, not guaranteed outcomes for each actual sample.
